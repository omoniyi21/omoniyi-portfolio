Buying your domain

Your domain is your address on the internet, like yourbusiness.com. This guide gets it into your name, on your account, and gives me just enough access to connect it to your site.

Time: about 15 minutesCost: usually $10 to $20 a year for a .comYou’ll need: a payment card

Before you start

Why you buy it, not me

The domain should belong to you, on an account you control. If you ever work with someone else, or I’m unreachable, your address never goes with them.

I get shared access, which lets me connect the domain to your site without your password. You can remove me at any time.

Part 1

Buy the domain

  1. Make a Namecheap account

    Go to Namecheap and sign up with your business email. Write your username down. You’ll use this account to renew each year.

  2. Search for your name

    Type the domain you want in the search bar. If the .com is taken, ask me before picking something else. A small change, like adding your city or a word like “shop,” is often better than a different ending.

  3. Add it to your cart for one year

    One year is fine. Turn auto-renew on, so the domain never lapses by accident.

  4. Skip the extras

    At checkout you’ll be offered hosting, email, security certificates and more. You don’t need any of them for your site: hosting and the security certificate are covered on my side. Leave the free domain privacy on, which keeps your home address out of public records.

  5. Pay and finish

    Once it’s paid, the domain shows up in your Domain List. That’s it for buying.

Part 2

Share access with me

This gives me permission to connect the domain to your site. I never see your password or payment details.

  1. Open your domain

    Sign in to Namecheap. In the left menu choose Domain List, then click Manage next to your domain.

  2. Go to Sharing & Transfer

    Open the Sharing & Transfer tab and find the Share Access section.

  3. Add me

    In the New Manager field, enter hello@omoniyistudio.com and click Add.

  4. Choose what I can change

    Give me access to the domain’s DNS and nameserver settings only. That’s all I need to connect your site. Leave everything else unchecked.

  5. Confirm with your password

    Namecheap asks for your password to confirm. This stays with you; I never see it. I’ll get an invitation and accept it on my side.

After that

What happens next

Send me a quick note that it’s done. I’ll connect the domain to your site. It can take up to a day to start working everywhere, and there’s nothing else for you to do.

Each year Namecheap will email you before it renews. If you ever want to remove my access, go back to Share Access and choose Remove next to my name.
